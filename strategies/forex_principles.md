## The Single Biggest Insight (Stop/Loss Room to Breathe)

> "It had taken me 6 or 7 years to realize I was awful at setting TP and SL. I stared too close to the Bars and didn't see the whole picture. I was setting SL 20 pips on an hour chart, now it's 40–60 pips. I use ATR × 2 or 3. Giving my trade room to breathe was the secret."

**Rule:** On the 1H chart, use **ATR × 2** minimum, **ATR × 3** when bars are clustering (ranging).
- Don't look at individual bars — look at them as a **cluster**
- A wider SL means you survive the noise before the move happens
- Even if price expands right after entry and looks like a "breakout against you" — keep the order
- 1:2 R:R is the sweet spot for this approach

## Entry Logic: Ranging vs Trending

### Ranging Market (price in a cluster)
1. Identify the cluster of bars at a price level
2. Wait for price to reach the **opposite end** of the range from your intended direction
3. Example: cluster is at a lower price, you want to go long → wait for price to reach the **top** of the range, then enter long
4. Use larger SL (ATR × 2–3) — you have breathing room even if range expands

### Trending Market (sharp momentum moves)
1. Never chase momentum entries
2. Wait for a **pullback** to a key level (MA, S/R, pivot)
3. Enter when pullback shows rejection candle
4. Do NOT manage the trade mid-run — greed costs more than it gives
5. If you cannot master trade management, just set TP and leave it alone

## Stop-Loss & Take-Profit Rules

| Setting | Rule |
|---|---|
| SL on 1H | ATR × 2 minimum (40–60 pips typical) |
| SL on 4H | ATR × 2–3 (larger = more room) |
| TP1 | 2× SL distance (1:2 R:R) |
| TP2 | 3× SL distance (optional) |
| Breakeven move | When in profit by ~2× SL distance, move SL to entry + a few pips (cover swap/commission) |
| Never | Move SL further away from entry to avoid being stopped out |
| Never | Move TP closer to lock in profits early (kills R:R edge) |

**Breakeven is a win.** If price reverses after you're up 150 pips and you moved to breakeven + 5 pips — that is a successful trade.

## Timeframe Hierarchy (Top-Down Analysis)

```
Weekly  → Determine long-term bias (bullish / bearish / ranging)
Daily   → Confirm direction, identify major S/R levels
4H      → Find trend structure, plot key levels
1H      → ENTRY timeframe — find the setup, place the order
```

- Never trade against the Daily and Weekly trend direction
- If Weekly is bearish, only look for shorts on the 1H
- Higher timeframe levels offer pivotal moments — when they break, something fundamental has changed
- Most professional traders only use 4H and Daily — fewer setups, higher quality

## Risk Management

| Rule | Default | Experienced Traders |
|---|---|---|
| Risk per trade | 2% of account | Up to 5% (only after proven edge) |
| Max open pairs | 5 | 3–5 |
| Daily outcome limit | 2 wins OR 2 losses = stop | Same |
| Annual risk limit | Never trade money you need | Never |

> "I risk 5% per trade. I'm not investing, I'm trading and I have earned the $30K I could potentially lose many times over." — Only after years of consistency.

**Start at 2%. Earn the right to increase risk through consistent results.**

## Tax & Profit Handling

**50/50 Rule for Profits:**
- When you realize a profit, set aside **50% immediately for taxes**
- Park tax money in a **money market fund** (e.g., Vanguard) — NOT invested in stocks
  - Reason: FIFO accounting makes stock investments with tax money unnecessarily complex
  - Money market earns yield while waiting (4–5% APY currently)
- The remaining 50% is yours to compound, withdraw, or reinvest

## What Successful Traders Do Differently

1. **They don't "lose"** — they collect feedback. A loss on a correct setup is still the right decision.
2. **They use price action** — always, as the foundation of any strategy
3. **They have a defined edge** — and follow it without deviation
4. **They don't try too hard** — 20–30 min/day max. Overanalyzing kills performance
5. **They think in dollar risk** — not just percentages. "Can I afford to lose $200 on this trade?"
6. **They don't need the money** — no pressure = no fear = no greed = better execution
7. **They walk away** — after wins AND losses. Emotions destroy objectivity
8. **They don't focus on win rate** — 50% win rate at 1:2 R:R is highly profitable
9. **They never gave up** — the only guaranteed way to fail is to quit

## The Edge That Works (Proven Over 3–4 Years)

```
Timeframe:   1H entry, 4H/Daily for direction
SL:          ATR × 2 (ranging) or ATR × 2–3 (trending pullback)
TP:          1:2 R:R minimum
Entry style: Wait for price to reach opposite end of range OR pullback in trend
Management:  Move to breakeven at +2× SL. Otherwise, leave it alone.
Indicators:  Price action primary. MFI as secondary confluence (underrated).
Risk:        2% paper → earn up to 5% after consistent profitability
```

## Indicators Worth Knowing (After Years of Testing)

- **MFI (Money Flow Index):** Surprisingly effective — underrated by most traders
- **ATR:** Essential for SL/TP sizing — use it on every trade
- **Price Action:** The foundation of everything — no indicator replaces it
- **Key Levels (S/R):** Plot from higher TFs, execute on lower TFs
- Most indicators are redundant — mastering fewer is better than knowing many

---

## What Doesn't Work (Hard-Earned Lessons)

- SL/TP that's too tight relative to the timeframe — biggest mistake most traders make
- Trading against the higher timeframe trend
- Managing winners out of greed (often costs more than it earns)
- Trading every day regardless of conditions
- Optimizing for high win rate instead of good R:R
- Overcomplicating — most veteran traders keep it surprisingly simple
- Revenge trading after losses (this is why the 2-outcome daily limit exists)
